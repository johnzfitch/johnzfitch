Research

I run the Transporter program, where I study the geometry inside transformers and build model architectures from the exceptional Jordan algebra (the 27-dimensional Albert algebra). I take constructions from algebra and geometry, turn them into models I can actually run, and test them. The questions I keep coming back to: what does a model hold on to, how does it change as it learns, and can its structure show us better ways to learn?

  • I built the Albert Engine (private repo), a tested numerical runtime for the Albert algebra. I use it to prototype sequence models, memory mechanisms, optimizers, and diagnostics that work with that geometry.
  • I study transformer representations and how optimizers behave numerically, and I run causal interventions on pretrained open models to see which geometric structure they actually depend on.
  • I test ideas with numerical checks and head-to-head comparisons under controlled conditions.
  • I write down my predictions before an experiment, mark each claim as proved, measured, or conjectured, and report the limits and the negative results next to the positive ones.

Engineering highlight (Jan 2026)

OpenAI Codex

Ghost in the Codex Machine

Codex release builds ran a hardening routine before main() that stripped LD_* and DYLD_* from the environment. Every tool subprocess inherited that environment, so CUDA and MKL couldn't find their libraries and fell back to slow paths without any error. I found the commit that turned it on, built a reproduction, and wrote it up in issue #8945. An OpenAI maintainer wrote the fix from my write-up, it shipped in rust-v0.80.0, and the release notes thank me by name.

Proof

  • Issue #8945 - Fix PR #8951
  • rust-v0.80.0 release notes

From my repro harness (numbers vary by setup)

  • MKL/BLAS: ~2.71s -> ~0.239s (11.3x)
  • CUDA: the libraries load again, so affected setups stop falling back to the CPU

Release notes excerpt:

"Special thanks to @johnzfitch for the detailed investigation and write-up in #8945."

Before this: TBD Liquids (2014–2023)

I started TBD Liquids in 2014 selling e-liquid I mixed by hand on Reddit's e-cigarette classifieds, and I ran it for nearly nine years. The mission was to help adults quit smoking cigarettes. The FDA regulated what we made as a tobacco product, and the work meant handling hazardous substances.

  • Built the brand, The Best Damn Liquids (federally trademarked), on made-to-order mixing, a panel of about 50 outside flavor testers, and a price ceiling of $11.99 per 30ml.
  • Served roughly 40,000 customers by 2019 and filled more than 200,000 orders, with a team of up to six.
  • Filed a Premarket Tobacco Product Application before FDA's September 2020 deadline, then spent years working through guidance that never committed to anything, FOIA requests included. FDA refused to file the application.
  • Said no to opportunities that pulled us away from the mission, even profitable ones. We closed in July 2023, in our strongest revenue year.

What it taught me:

  • How to keep going for years when a regulator won't give you a straight answer.
  • How to run manufacturing, hiring, and fulfillment day to day, and how to handle a crisis.
  • How to work under public scrutiny, for nearly nine years of it. I stand behind the work and I'm comfortable defending it on the record.
